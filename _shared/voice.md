# Repository voice

This is the starter voice for specifications, documentation, issues, pull requests, summaries, and other repository-facing communication. Accept or revise it during [`../setup/questionnaire.md`](../setup/questionnaire.md); do not treat it as repository-specific until then.

Accuracy, clarity, and evidence matter more than sounding impressive. Product-facing brand voice belongs in the relevant Product Specification, not here.

## Lead with the answer

- State the outcome first, then provide the detail needed to understand, decide, or verify it.
- When the response is longer than a few sentences, begin with a one- or two-line summary.
- Give the reader enough information to assess the work in the first response.
- Do not let brevity hide material changes, risks, assumptions, uncertainty, failures, skipped checks, validation results, or required human decisions.

## Write plainly

- Use common, precise words and short sentences.
- Prefer active voice.
- Use one term consistently and define it when first needed.
- Avoid idioms, ambiguous pronouns, unnecessary abbreviations, and long noun clusters.
- Write procedures as ordered steps with one action per step.

## Explain decisions

- Separate observed facts, assumptions, recommendations, and human decisions.
- Do not present a technically true fragment as the whole answer when a missing qualifier would change the decision.
- Tie material recommendations to the canonical Project intention. Say explicitly when a recommendation departs from it.
- Name contradictions between goals, specifications, evidence, and implementation instead of smoothing them over.
- For a material decision, explain the options, trade-offs, what can go wrong, and who owns the choice.
- At a material human gate, explain both the local reason for the recommendation and how it supports the overall Project intention.
- Use a concrete Project example when an abstract explanation could be misunderstood.
- Ask one decision question at a time.

Routine progress updates do not need to repeat the full rationale. Cognitive alignment is reached when the human understands and approves the relevant artifact and its trade-offs, not when every sentence is acknowledged.

## Do not sell

- Do not praise the plan, analysis, or implementation.
- Avoid promotional or theatrical wording such as “game-changing,” “powerful,” “exciting,” “best-in-class,” “seamless,” “elegant solution,” and “great news.”
- Use words such as “robust” only when naming the specific reliability property.
